Scotland: Highlands, Castles, and Legends - 7-Day Diary

The land of clans, mysterious castles, and wild landscapes. Our trip through Scotland was an epic adventure between the vibrant culture of Edinburgh and the majestic, remote Highlands. Here is our 7-day diary.

Day 1 - Edinburgh: The Royal Mile

We arrived in Edinburgh and checked into the historic The Balmoral Hotel. The day was dedicated to exploring the Royal Mile, the main artery of the old town connecting Edinburgh Castle to the Palace of Holyroodhouse. We climbed up to Edinburgh Castle for a stunning view of the city. Dinner was a traditional Haggis, Neeps and Tatties at a local pub.

Day 2 - Arthur’s Seat and Museum Hopping

We started the day with an invigorating hike to the top of Arthur’s Seat, an ancient extinct volcano offering 360-degree panoramic views. It was a great workout! In the afternoon, we visited the National Gallery of Scotland and the National Museum of Scotland. We ended the day with a “dram” (shot) of single malt whisky at one of the city’s many whisky bars.

Day 3 - Journey to the Highlands and Loch Ness

We left Edinburgh and drove north towards the Highlands. The scenery changed dramatically to towering mountains and shimmering lochs. We arrived at the infamous Loch Ness, where we took a boat ride hoping to spot the legendary creature! We also visited the ruins of Urquhart Castle on the lake’s shore. The night was spent at a cozy countryside hotel near Fort Augustus.

Day 4 - The Magical Isle of Skye

A full day on the Isle of Skye, connected to the mainland by a bridge. The landscapes here look otherworldly. We hiked up to the Old Man of Storr, a striking pinnacle rock formation, and passed through the bogs and cliffs of the Quiraing. The weather was rainy, but it only added to the dramatic mood. We stayed at the Kinloch Lodge for a well-deserved treat.

Day 5 - The Stunning and Somber Glencoe

On the journey back south, we passed through the valley of Glencoe. It’s one of Scotland’s most photographed landscapes, with steep mountains and low-hanging clouds. It’s also the site of the tragic Massacre of Glencoe of 1692, giving the area a somber and moving atmosphere. We stopped in Fort William for lunch and a quick recharge before continuing the trip.

Day 6 - Glasgow: Architecture and Music

We spent the penultimate day in Glasgow, Scotland’s largest city, known for its Victorian architecture and vibrant music scene. We explored Glasgow Cathedral and the Glasgow Necropolis, an impressive Victorian cemetery overlooking the city. We also visited the Kelvingrove Art Gallery and Museum. Glasgow has a more industrial and modern vibe compared to Edinburgh.

Day 7 - Stirling Castle and Farewell

On our last day, we visited Stirling Castle, historically crucial and often referred to as the “key to Scotland.” It’s one of the most important castles in the country. From there, we visited the National Wallace Monument, which celebrates the Scottish hero William Wallace. It was the perfect way to end our journey, taking in one last dose of Scottish history before heading to the airport. The Highlands and their legends will stay with us forever!
